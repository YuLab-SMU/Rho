# Claim calibration and Rho positioning

Use this reference to construct a defensible research explanation for Rho.

## Argument spine

Organize a positioning brief as:

`consequential problem -> existing explanations -> unresolved distinction -> Rho proposal -> mechanism -> evidence -> boundary`

A framework description becomes a research contribution only when it identifies a problem, explains a mechanism, and names an observable consequence. A list of interface features or a successful demonstration does not establish effectiveness.

## Claim types

Label every claim with one of these types:

- `reported_observation`: a source reports a behavior, score or event.
- `association`: two measured properties co-occur without an identified causal design.
- `causal_effect`: a comparison supports a causal interpretation with stated assumptions.
- `conceptual_proposal`: a distinction or framework proposed by an author or by Rho.
- `human_use`: a participant completed, understood or recovered from a task.
- `scientific_validity`: an analysis or result passed an appropriate domain check.
- `normative_recommendation`: a design or research choice recommended with reasons.

Store `claim_status` separately. Use `supported_by_source`, `observed_in_rho`, `proposed`, `tested`, `unresolved`, `contradicted` and `access_limited`.

## Rho-specific positioning questions

Ask these questions while comparing adjacent work:

1. What is the shared object of work? Files, messages, notebook state, tasks, artifacts, decisions or a combination?
2. Who can enter, understand, change and continue the work?
3. Which state survives a participant or session boundary?
4. What evidence connects a purpose to an action, result, decision and next step?
5. Which operations are native to a component and which are composed by the application?
6. What does the evaluation measure? Task success, continuity, comprehension, recovery, scientific validity or something else?
7. Which Rho claim is genuinely new, and which is an integration or a tested application of existing ideas?

Use these questions to build comparison rows. Do not declare novelty until the comparison set and its scope are visible.

## Evidence language

Use wording that matches the evidence:

- “The paper reports …” for an author-reported result.
- “The Rho episode observed …” for a bounded run.
- “This suggests …” only when the inferential bridge is stated.
- “We propose …” for an untested framework or mechanism.
- “The evidence does not yet establish …” for an open claim.

Every strong, disputed, causal, quantitative or novelty claim needs a source locator, a warrant and a limitation in the claim register.

## Positioning output

The positioning brief should include:

- the target reader and the concrete problem;
- adjacent systems and evaluation traditions;
- a comparison table with source locators;
- two or three candidate Rho contributions;
- the evidence already available for each candidate;
- the experiment required to distinguish them;
- a boundary paragraph that names the work Rho does not yet support.

Keep candidate contributions separate until the evaluation map identifies a claim that can be tested.
