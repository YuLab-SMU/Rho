The Graph as linked levels ​

Updated 2026-11-11.

Question ​

On 2026-11-11 the user judged the Graph shallow. Recording every action of an Agent is needed, but a drawing that maps those actions one to one shows no value by itself and becomes a clutter as sessions accumulate. The meaning layer drawn above it was almost isolated from the behaviour below. Levels should have connection points, so that behaviour and meaning can be linked and higher abstractions built on them. From this page a person or an Agent should see at a glance what the workspace did, open it level by level, and reach the raw behaviour. They added that nothing should be left out for size ("应该不设上限"), and that the page need not be advanced at once.

  1. Can one screen say what was done in a project of several sessions?
  2. Is every upper node a fold of lower nodes it can list, down to the recorded steps?
  3. Does a statement in the notes lead to the files it rests on and to the steps that made them, and to the step that wrote it?

Observed before ​

Measured on the real records of the PA14 quorum-sensing project (5 conversations, 11 messages, 237 recorded steps) with a throwaway probe:

  • The behaviour graph had 188 nodes. The Graph drew at most 160 and left out the oldest message past that, so this project already lost 1 of its 11 messages.
  • Messages of 50 and 66 steps were each one row of cards. Nothing stood between a message and its steps.
  • The meaning layer was a second drawing on the same canvas. It met the behaviour only through "kept as" and "written in" edges, and from a finding the old edges reached a median of 54 actions across up to 4 sessions: too many to be an answer to "what is this based on".
  • The page read the 6 newest conversations and the 8 newest messages of each.

Changed ​

One model of levels (src/work-layers.ts, no DOM). The workspace holds lines of work, a line holds the person's messages, a message holds activities, an activity holds recorded steps. Each node lists its children, and trajectory returns every step under any node in order.

  • A step is one entry of the conversation record, with its command, output and ending.
  • An activity is a stretch of steps of one kind: looking around, cells in the live session, a script written and run in rounds, the notes, the answer. The Agent's own words open the activity that follows them. Its name is counted from its steps ("Wrote analysis.R and ran it twice"), not written by a model.
  • A line of work is a section of the project notes with the conversation it names, or a conversation the notes do not cover. Conversations that made the same files are one line. A conversation that changed no file and has no section stands below the work.

Three kinds of connection.

  • Membership, above.
  • Touches: a step read, edited, wrote or ran a file, or a file changed on disk while a command ran. Each keeps its basis (reported, named in a command, seen on disk). A line drawn to a closed node is the sum of the touches under it (links, shownAs), so opening a node moves each line to the deeper node it was recorded on.
  • Statements: a finding or choice in the notes is tied to the files it names, and to the step that wrote it, found by the kept version of the notes that first holds it and the tool call that kept that version. support walks back from those files through the steps that made them and what those steps read. A line of work builds on another when it used files the other made (flows).

No cap. The 160-node limit and the limits on conversations and messages are gone. The page reads every session, four at a time, and each whole in pages of 50 messages; it reads every kept version of the notes, six at a time. What is drawn is what is open. A statement first found after a notes version that could not be read is not traced.

One drawing (src/layers-layout.ts, web/graph.tsx). Closed, a project is its lines of work in the order they began, each a card with its question, newest figure and counts, joined where one builds on another. Open, a line reads left to right: Used, Work, Made, Concluded. The work column opens in place, a message into activities and an activity into steps. Several files taken from another line are one card that leads there. Choosing a statement lights the files it rests on and the work behind them, and "Show the step that wrote it" opens the levels down to that step, whose details show the notes as kept after it. Choosing a file lists what happened to it step by step and what is concluded from it. Search covers every level and opens what it finds. The timeline, replay, following and the Agent's flag work on the same nodes; the flag sits on the deepest open node that stands for the step the Agent is on.

The separate meaning layer, its toggle and the per-message lane layout were removed (src/meaning-graph.ts, layoutGraph, laneLayout). A command that only looks at a script (cat, head, grep, sed -n and the like) no longer counts as running it (ranFiles).

Runs ​

PA14 quorum sensing, real records, real Chrome. A copy in /tmp served by a disposable service; evidence in work/graph-layers-20261111/run6.

  • The page says: "3 lines of work, 11 messages, 237 recorded steps. The notes conclude 31 things from it." Three cards carry the three questions with their figures; two conversations that changed nothing stand below. Swarming and Co-culture each lead into RNA-seq ("builds on 4 files", "builds on 2 files").
  • 237 steps fold into 37 activities (the largest 30 steps), 11 messages, 5 lines.
  • Of 31 findings and choices, 29 name a file a recorded step made; 2 name none. Behind each of the 29 stand 6 to 14 steps (median 12), all in its own line of work.
  • One finding read: rests on results/rnaseq_mutant_overlap.csv, "12 recorded steps in 3 activities", "Written by Claude Code at 12:34:24, first in version 16 of 23 of the notes". Its step, "Edited project-notes.md", opened with the notes as kept after it.

The same records repeated to 25 conversations (55 messages, 1,185 steps; work/graph-layers-20261111/many3). The lines were drawn 0.2 s after the click, every session was read by 0.4 s, and a line opened in 0.07 s, with the service on the same machine. The first run of this check showed three defects, since fixed: the page opened on one card in the middle of an empty canvas, lines between rows ran through other cards, and the timeline's lane names collapsed. Past 8 lines of work a closed line is now its name and counts, so the 15 lines and 10 other conversations were on one screen with readable names; the timeline shows 6 lanes and scrolls the rest, newest first.

Checks ​

  • test/work-layers.test.mjs: activities from a recorded message, lines and what they build on, a statement's support and the step that wrote it, lifted lines, the layout, and the shipped example/pa14-qs (every recorded step is in the graph; each named statement rests on a file a recorded step made, behind it at most 40 steps).
  • npm run acceptance:graph: 9 of 9 with a controlled peer that runs the real Rscript. scripts/review-evidence-acceptance.mjs passed with the Graph opening a version-bound review.
  • All tests: 347 passed, 3 skipped, none failed (350). npm run check:contracts passed.

Limits ​

  • A change seen on disk while a command ran says when, not that the command made it.
  • The record does not say which data a script read. A script's inputs appear only when a step read them or a command named them, so a statement's support can stop at the script.
  • A notes section keeps all the messages of its conversation, including a later message about another section's subject.
  • Activity names are counted, and a line without a notes section is named by its conversation's title. No model writes a summary of a node yet.
  • The browser reads every session when the page opens. Nothing is precomputed, and the 25-conversation run was on one machine with a local service.
  • An Agent cannot read these levels yet: they are built in the browser.
  • Past 8 lines the closed cards lose their figure. With many more lines than one screen holds, the page opens on full rows from the top; there is no level between the workspace and its lines.
  • Only the builder has read these screens.

Many conversations ​

Question (2026-11-12). The user sent two screens of the Graph for a real project and judged the design ugly and the timeline under it of no use once conversations pile up.

Observed before. A copy of that project in /tmp with its session records: 16 Claude Code conversations, 61 messages and 3,951 recorded steps over five days, read at 2000 × 1005 in real Chrome (work/graph-timeline-20261112/before).

  • The timeline gave each conversation a lane: 18 lanes and 2,968 blocks in a strip that shows 6 lanes. Twelve lanes were out of sight at any moment, and since one conversation works at a time, each lane in sight was empty for most of its width. The axis gave times without days over five days (12:58, 22:08, 06:04), and a busy stretch was a pile of blocks, rings and diamonds on top of each other.
  • 13 of the 16 conversations had changed the same files and were one line of work, named by the first of them; its card said nothing of the other 12. Opened, it was one frame of 1,008 × 13,138 px with 56 messages in a column.
  • Two of the three cards quoted <pasted_content id="…"> as what was asked, the tag a pasted passage arrives in. Every card carried the label "Line of work", an icon in a tinted square, counts as pills and a shadow under a border.

Changed.

  • The timeline is drawn where work happened (src/timeline-layout.ts, web/timeline.tsx). A conversation is a named piece from its first message to its last, a piece again when it is taken up after other work, and pieces that do not overlap, names included, share a row, so rows follow how many conversations ran close together and not how many there are (names shorten before the strip takes more than timelineSizes.rows, 4; further rows scroll). A message is a band; its steps are columns stacked by kind (ran, edited, read, changed on disk, versions kept), their height by count on a log scale, with a dot under a column that holds a failed, refused or unknown step. Pauses over 30 s fold to 4 to 14 px by length and a stretch is as wide as its steps and duration ask. The axis names each day and gives times within it.
  • The timeline zooms (the buttons, a pinch or ⌘/Ctrl with the wheel, a double click on a conversation) to 48 times its width and scrolls; names stay in sight while their piece is. Hovering names the conversation, the message, when and how long, and the steps under the pointer. A click on a message chooses it in the graph, a click on a name chooses the conversation, and what is chosen in the graph is marked in the timeline. Replay is dragged on the axis.
  • A conversation is a level between a line of work and its messages (talksOf). A line of several conversations is named by the first "and N more", lists them on its card with their day and messages, and heads each one's messages when open; a conversation has its own details beside the canvas.
  • A message, conversation or line is named by its words without the pasted-content tags (plainAsk).
  • Cards carry a title, one line of facts and what they stand for; the label, the tinted icon, the pills and the resting shadow are gone. A line that builds on another is drawn at the height of the cards' heads, or over the cards between them.
  • A graph too tall for one screen that fits across the page down to 66% opens across it from the top, and only one too wide for that opens small.

After, same records (work/graph-timeline-20261112/after-*).

  • At 2000 × 1005 all 16 conversations are in sight in 4 rows as 19 named pieces, none scrolled away, under an axis of five named days; the strip is 222 px high where the lanes took 238 px. The graph opens at 100% and the large line's card lists its 13 conversations.
  • At 1440 × 900 and 1280 × 800 the same 4 rows hold, names shortened where the next piece begins, the legend on its own line; the graph opens at 85% and 72%, where fitting the whole at 1280 × 800 had given 44%.
  • The shipped example (3 conversations, 6 messages) is 2 rows, and its cards keep their figures.

Checks. test/timeline-layout.test.mjs (pauses fold and a moment goes to its place and back, a busy stretch is wider than a long quiet one, conversations in turn share a row and overlapping ones do not, 16 in turn stay within the rows, a resumed conversation is a second piece, every step is in one column of its conversation by kind, the axis names each day without labels running into each other) and test/work-layers.test.mjs (conversations of a line, a pasted passage named by its words). All tests: 366 passed, 3 skipped, none failed (369). npm run acceptance:graph 9 of 9 on the changed screens.

Limits.

  • Lines of work are still joined by the files conversations changed, so a project whose conversations all touch its notes or README is one large line. The card and the open line now say which conversations it holds; the grouping is unchanged.
  • An opened line of 56 messages is still one long column, headed by conversation.
  • Past 4 rows the strip scrolls; this project took 4 at each width tried, and no run has needed more.
  • The colours of the kinds are the application's, not checked for colour-blind separation; a failed step is a dot as well as a colour.
  • Only the builder has read these screens; the user's judgment of the new form is open.
